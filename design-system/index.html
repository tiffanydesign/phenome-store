01

Typeface

Two faces, and they divide by job rather than by rank. helvetica-neue-lt-pro sets everything that is language. new-science-mono sets everything that is a number — a price, a reading, a token value on this page. Both come from one Adobe kit, shared with phenome-digital-twin.

The system is sans-serif. There is no serif in it, anywhere.

Not as a default and not as a fallback: both font stacks end on a generic sans — sans-serif and monospace — and New Science Mono is a fixed-pitch sans, in the system because a column of prices has to line up on the decimal, not because it has serifs. The one route a serif could still take is an <svg><text> with no font-family, which falls back to the platform UA face and lands on Times New Roman under Windows. There are none on the site, and a guard rule names that element so there cannot be.

On a dark ground

Phenome Blue is 1.8:1 on the navy and unreadable, so the dark ground carries its own lighter blue — #8fb8ea, 7.6:1 — and secondary text takes Blue 200 rather than Ink 400, which lands at 3.97:1 inside a card and fails.

04

The ground

A rimless pane holds its edge on its drop alone, and a drop over flat near-white is the weakest reading this material has. So the page is given structure instead of the pane being given an edge back: a 150° three-stop wash, two brand pools and two very large corner lobes, fixed to the window so the panes travel over it.

  • 01Cyan pool, top right#30b0cd 10%
  • 02Brand pool, bottom left#203a85 7%
  • 03Corner lobe, top left#c4daf7 45%
  • 04Corner lobe, bottom right#d2e2f8 40%
  • 05Base wash, 150°#eef3f9 → #e5edf7 → #dce7f4

The lobes are dropped below 900px and wherever there is no pointer. iOS composites a fixed background on every scroll frame, and a phone has no room for them anyway — the linear wash alone carries the hue there.

05

Glass surfaces

Three properties, and there is no fourth. Change the ground under the panes below and the reason the tiers exist becomes visible: over the field a 42% pane is a surface, over a quiet ground it stops being one, and over a photograph only the top two tiers still hold their own boundary.

Switch to Navy and the labels do not all invert together. They flip at 42% and at 18% and hold their navy ink in between, because a translucent white fill over a dark ground crosses from reading as a dark surface to reading as a light one somewhere between 42% and 50% — measured, not felt. That crossover is the whole reason section 08 exists.

A recess is not thicker glass.

It is one flat blue-grey wash and nothing else — no inset, no drop, no rim, and a thinner blur than a pane, which is the opposite of what the three previous doctrines all said and the reason this one is shorter than any of them.

06

Elevation & motion

With no inner term left there is nothing to invert, so the material has exactly one move: the pane comes further off the page, or settles back onto it. Hover does not brighten the fill — brightening a translucent fill reads as glass going opaque, which is the one thing it cannot do.

620 rather than 240 for the reveal, because a band arriving is not a click: at 240 it snaps and reads as a glitch; at 620 it reads as weight.

08

Inverted contexts

The one rule that has to be checked by hand every time a surface is added, because nothing on this project measures colour automatically. A control whose ink is already white cannot take the light chip fill — it lands at 1.1:1, and the label does not fade, it vanishes.

Not discovered one page at a time. The same failure was found three times while this system was being applied — on a dark product card, on a glass buy-card over film, and on a call-to-action over a moving photograph — and each time the cause was identical: a blanket pane fill reaching a component that states its own white ink.
