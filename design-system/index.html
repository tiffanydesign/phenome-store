Typeface

Helvetica Neue LT Pro, set site wide by --font-sans.

One family, and no second one. Display, heading, body and figure all come from this face. A page that wants a different voice takes it from size and from ink, never from a serif. Two pages carried Newsreader until 2026-09-16, Science for its numerals and its italics and the App page for its display line and its pull quote. Both are back on the one family, and the Google Fonts request that fetched the second face is gone from both.

Type scale

Twelve rungs, and the smallest is 15. Tracking is negative at display sizes, zero at body, +0.04em on the overline. The bottom two rungs were 12 and 14 until 2026-09-17; they carried the navigation, the footer, every product card and every catalogue description, and at those sizes none of it could be read comfortably. Nothing below 15 appears in page copy now. The only type on the site still smaller is inside a drawn interface, where it is a picture of a phone screen rather than a line of text.

The statement ladder

The twelve rungs above say what sizes exist. This says which one a line gets, and it is the Band page’s answer, taken for the whole store on 2026-09-16. Four rungs carry every statement on a page, and the rule that picks between them is about what the line is doing, not about how important it feels.

  • Display, .ph-displayclamp(44px, 8.4vw, 132px), 700, −.035em, two lines, two tone. A section’s whole argument. Section 09 has the rest.
  • Solid, .ph-solidclamp(34px, 4.4vw, 60px), 600, −.020em, a section’s name. One or two words, one ink, never two tone.
  • Name, .ph-nameclamp(32px, 3.2vw, 48px), 600, −.020em, the product or the page itself, at the head of a decision column.
  • Promise, .ph-promiseclamp(20px, 1.6vw, 24px), 600, −.010em, the one line under a name that says what the thing is for.
  • Support, .ph-support17/1.55, 400, the lede, the description, the caption. Everything that explains rather than announces.
  • Field, .ph-field16/22, name at 600, value at 400 beside it on one baseline. Every choice in a decision column.

The overline is a promise line

.eyebrow was 12/16 at +.06em, which is the setting a capitalised overline takes. The letters were sentence case but the typesetting was the all caps device, and the page read that way. It is 15/20 at −.005em in Blue 500 now: a size up, tracking back to normal, and the same job the Band’s one blue line does under its product name. The 12 rung is still on the ladder for the places that genuinely need a caption.

The floor

15px is the smallest size any word a reader is meant to read may be set in. Not the smallest on the ladder, the smallest permitted: 12 and 14 stay for captions and for the metadata beside a control, and nothing under 15 carries a sentence. Audited and corrected on 2026-09-16, when the basket was still setting its plan line, its fine print and its add button at 12 and 13, and the mega menu its note at 12.

Two things are outside this rule and neither of them is copy. A counter inside a badge, such as the cart’s own 15px circle, is a numeral in a shape rather than a line of text. And the phone mockups on the app pages are pictures of an interface, not the interface: their 9 and 10px labels are drawn at the scale a rendered screenshot would be, and raising them would make the mock read as a poster of an app rather than as an app.

A page that wants a whole section quieter takes it from the ink, --ph-tone-quiet, not from a size below the floor. That is what the fourth ink is for.

Text inks, what carries colour, and what does not

The swatches above are the palette. These four are the whole of it that text is allowed to use, and they are the Band page’s, promoted on 2026-09-16. Nothing outside this list carries type colour. A heading that picks its own grey, or a paragraph that borrows the link blue, has left the system.

  • --ph-tone-head, Ink 900Every heading, every figure, every price. The default, and the only ink a number is set in.
  • --ph-tone-promise, Blue 500The promise line, and nothing else. This is the one line on a light page set in the brand colour, which is what keeps it meaning something.
  • --ph-tone-body, Ink 600Ledes, descriptions, captions, the value beside a field name. Everything that explains.
  • --ph-tone-quiet, Ink 400Legends, and the small print under a control.
  • --ph-tone-lead, Blue 200Not a fifth ink: the heading ink held back, for the display’s first line. White at 55% on a dark ground.

On a dark ground the whole ladder inverts under one class, .on-night — head to white, promise and body to Blue 200 — so a section that changes its ground changes one class rather than nine colours.

Display headline

The largest statement a page is allowed to make, drawn first for the Band page and now carried by the kit as .ph-display. Two lines stacked, the first held back, the second at full contrast, so one sentence carries its own emphasis without a rule, a colour block or a second weight.

On dark grounds the lead line is white at 55%. On light grounds it is --phenome-blue-200 against the ink. Nothing else changes between the two.

When it is solid instead

A statement long enough to break over two lines is set two tone. A single word, or a line short enough to read at a glance, is set solid in the Specifications ink and takes no lead line, because a colour shift across three words reads as an error rather than emphasis.

Modifiers

  • .is-inkLight grounds. Ink 900 with a blue 200 lead.
  • .is-evenBoth lines at one size. Contrast alone separates them.
  • .is-leftLeft aligned rather than centred.
  • .is-autoTakes the alignment of whatever contains it, for the heroes that have already decided that question.
  • .is-heroThe hero step, clamp(38px, 5.6vw, 84px), and the hero’s own top margin. Narrows to 72px inside a two column .hero-split.

Size is set by the section that holds it, not by the component. The kit ships the ceiling, clamp(44px, 8.4vw, 132px); pages step it down where the headline sits beside other content.

The pair is a block, not a flex column, and each line is a block level span. That is what lets .is-auto exist: alignment is one inherited property rather than a rule the component imposes on its container.

Where it is used

Settled 2026-09-16: every page on this site takes this pair for its largest statement, and the hero heading is the first of them. Forty eight hero headings moved onto it in one pass; the ones that stayed on h1.hero-title stayed because they are a single short line, which is this ladder’s solid rung and the correct treatment for them.

  • Heroes.ph-display.is-ink.is-auto.is-hero on a light ground; drop .is-ink over a dark hero or a film.
  • Section statements.ph-display with the section’s own size, as on devices/band, store/phenometech-ring and supplements/nad.
  • Section namesSolid. .ph-solid / .bd-h2 / h2.sec-title. Never two tone.
  • The one exclusiondevices/ring-lab. It is a design laboratory with its own condensed italic display face and its own scale, kept whole on purpose, and it is excluded from this rule by name.

Two copy rules that travel with it

  • No connectorsNo hyphen, en dash or em dash anywhere in copy, and no ampersand standing in for “and”. A dash that joined two halves of a sentence becomes a comma. The only hyphens left on the site are inside names that carry one: L-serine, Gly-NAC, X-linked, Tay-Sachs, UC-II, GLP-1.
  • No capitalsSentence case everywhere, enforced by *, *::before, *::after { text-transform: none } in phenome-glass.css § 2c. Acronyms that are genuinely capitals stay: DNA, NAD, GDPR, ISO, HRV.
10

Buy and Add to cart

Two labels, and the difference between them is not a matter of taste. One of them is navigation and the other one changes the basket. They were used interchangeably until 2026-09-16, when “Add to cart” had spread onto forty five controls that add nothing to anything.

The rule

  • BuyA link that goes to a product’s buy page. It is what the home tiles, the store cards, the testing pages and every explore page say, because that is all any of them do.
  • Add to cartA control on a buy page that puts the configured item in the basket. Nothing else on the site is allowed to carry this label.
  • Checkout nowThe one exception, on devices/band: it adds and goes straight on to the checkout, so it is the second half of the pair rather than a second copy of the first.

The test

Read the href. If it points at a product page, the label is Buy. If it points at /store/cart/ or runs a cart script, the label is Add to cart. An explore page never says Add to cart, because an explore page has nothing configured to add.

The timeline

The shape this store reaches for whenever it has to answer when does this start working. A rail fills with the scroll; every stage the line passes lights and stays lit, so what the reader watches is an accumulation rather than a selection. Drawn first for NAD, promoted to the kit on 2026-09-16 as .ph-tl.

It was promoted because it had been drawn twice. NAD had this. The Ring had a stepper wearing the same clothes: four buttons, one lit at a time, the other three dimmed, and a fill that jumped from dot to dot. The second one says pick a stage; the first says it builds, which is the argument both sections were making. There is one of them now and both pages take it.

The parts

  • .ph-tlThe two column grid. One column at 1023 and under.
  • .ph-tl-stepsThe rail column. Carries data-ph-tl, which is what shared.js looks for and what opts the stages in to dimming.
  • .ph-tl-railA hairline with an <i> inside it. The fill scales rather than grows, so a frame of scroll costs a composite and not a layout.
  • .ph-tl-stepOne stage. Gains .on and keeps it. Never a button.
  • .ph-tl-pillNight 1, Week 1, Month 3. Navy chip, white text, inside the stage’s heading.
  • .ph-tl-mediaThe collage. .ph-tl-big spans both columns at 16:10 and holds a caption; .ph-tl-sm is 4:3 and .ph-tl-round is a circle. Add data-ph-float to drift a tile.
  • .ph-tl-noteThe chip under the rail, for the one instruction the section ends on.

Rules for reusing it

Change the words and the pictures. Do not change the interaction. A page supplies stages, bullets, three images and a caption, and gets the rest. Nothing about the fill, the dots, the cumulative lighting or the drift is a per page decision, and a page that wants a different one of those is asking for a second component rather than a setting on this one.

  • The stages are not selectableNobody picks month three, they arrive at it. Buttons would add four tab stops in front of text that is already read straight through.
  • The big tile takes a film tooThe tile does not care what it holds. A portrait film covers rather than contains, or it is a strip between two margins.
  • StillnessUnder prefers-reduced-motion every stage is lit and the rail is full. A progress line frozen at zero reads as broken.
