THE BASICS

What a gene is

A gene is a small section of your DNA that tells your body how to grow, heal, and function — from how your cells repair to how your metabolism and immunity work. Together, your genes form the complete blueprint of your health. Everyone’s genes differ, shaping everything from inherited traits to performance potential.

WHAT YOUR DNA REVEALS

What you can learn from your genes

By analysing your genes, we can read patterns that explain how your body works and what it needs. Insights span nine areas:

Nutrient processing

How efficiently your body absorbs and uses vitamins, minerals, and proteins.

Stress & inflammation

How you manage cortisol and recover from physical or emotional strain.

Detox & antioxidant capacity

How your body neutralises toxins and defends against oxidative stress.

Metabolism & energy

How your body converts food into usable energy, and whether you tend toward faster or slower energy use.

Hormones & sleep

Melatonin, cortisol, and circadian patterns that shape rest and daily rhythm.

Mental health & cognition

Dopamine and serotonin regulation affecting focus, mood, and resilience.

Health-condition risk factors

Variations linked to cardiovascular disease, diabetes, inflammation, and lipid balance.

Cellular repair & ageing

DNA repair, telomere maintenance, and how your cells renew over time.

Genetic resilience & longevity

How your biology maintains stability and adapts with age.

WHY IT MATTERS

Why it matters for longevity

Many biological imbalances begin developing long before any signs appear. Knowing how your genes influence these systems lets you act early — building a personalised approach to nutrition, activity, and prevention that supports long-term vitality.

WHOLE GENOME SEQUENCING

Most tests read a fraction. We read all of it.

Every cell in your body contains DNA — an instruction manual written in about three billion letters. Most genetic tests read only small parts of it. Whole Genome Sequencing reads all of it, so we can see every piece of genetic information that makes you who you are. It also means your data can be re-examined as the science advances.
