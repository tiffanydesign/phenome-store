THE BASICS

What the microbiome is

Your microbiome is the community of trillions of microbes that live in and on your body — unique to you, shaped by genetics, diet, environment, and lifestyle. Far from passive passengers, they work alongside your organs every day.

What your microbes do for you

Digest food

Break down what you eat so your body can absorb it.

Produce vitamins

Make vitamins and other compounds your body needs.

Train immunity

Help teach your immune system what to defend against.

Signal mood

Send signals that influence mood and stress through the gut-brain axis.

Balance is everything

Balanced

Diverse and stable, your microbes do their jobs well.

Disrupted

Knocked off balance by processed diets, stress, or antibiotics.

EXPLORE

The three core microbiomes

The body hosts many microbial communities, but three have the greatest influence on health and longevity.

Gut

The largest and most diverse. It breaks down food, produces vitamins like B12 and K, trains the immune system, and communicates with the brain to influence mood, sleep, and stress.

Oral

The gateway to the digestive and respiratory systems. It guards against pathogens and keeps the mouth in balance; disruption is linked to gum disease and wider effects on heart and metabolic health.

Vaginal

Central to women’s health. Dominated by protective Lactobacillus species that maintain an acidic, protective environment, supporting fertility, hormonal balance, and comfort across life stages.

WHY IT MATTERS

Gut microbiome testing: why it matters for digestive health.

The gut microbiome is a central regulator of human health, influencing digestion, immune function, metabolism, energy levels, and brain–gut signalling linked to mood and cognitive clarity. When balanced, these systems function optimally. When disrupted (microbial dysbiosis), measurable imbalances can affect multiple aspects of daily health.
