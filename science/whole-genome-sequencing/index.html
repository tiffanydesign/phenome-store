How your genome is read

From a saliva sample to a clinical grade report, every step is quality controlled in an accredited lab.

01Sample collection

You give a simple saliva sample at home with the prepaid kit. DNA is stabilised for transport and logged the moment it reaches the lab.

02Library prep and sequencing

Your DNA is fragmented, barcoded and sequenced on next generation platforms at 30× average depth, every region of the genome is read many times over for confidence.

03Bioinformatics and variant calling

Billions of short reads are aligned to a reference genome. Our pipeline calls single nucleotide variants, insertions, deletions and structural changes, then filters for quality.

04Clinical interpretation

Variants are annotated against curated clinical databases and reviewed against ACMG guidance, then written up in plain English with clear, actionable next steps.

Why whole genome, not a chip

The genotyping chips behind most consumer DNA kits read a tiny, fixed set of positions. Whole genome sequencing reads everything.

Genotyping chip

  • ✕Reads ~0.02% of your genome, a fixed panel of preset positions.
  • ✕Blind to rare and novel variants that aren’t on the chip.
  • ✕Can’t detect most structural or insertion/deletion changes.
  • ✕Fixed forever, no reanalysis as science advances.

Whole genome sequencing

  • ✓Reads all 3.2 billion base pairs at 30× depth.
  • ✓Finds rare, novel and clinically actionable variants.
  • ✓Captures SNVs, indels and structural variation.
  • ✓Reanalysed for life from the same sample, no retest.
