What isA gene?

A gene is a small part of your DNA that tells your body how to grow, heal, and function. Genes control everything from how your cells repair to how your metabolism and immunity work. Together, your genes create the complete blueprint of your health.

What do genes tell us

Your genes are like your body’s instruction code. They reveal how your body processes nutrients, adapts to stress, balances hormones and responds to exercise and sleep.

By analysing your genes, we can uncover patterns that explain how your body functions and what it needs to thrive. Gaining these insights helps you make informed choices that support your overall balance and vitality.

Genetic data varies from person to person, influencing everything from performance potential to inherited traits. To see how your own genetics are analysed across different areas, choose a test below and read the science behind each one.

What you can learnFrom your genes

Nine readings drawn from the same sample, each one explaining a different part of how your body runs.

01

How your body processes nutrients

Your genes influence how efficiently your body absorbs and uses nutrients such as vitamins, minerals, and proteins. These variations affect enzyme activity and nutrient transport, shaping how your body converts food into energy.

By analysing these genetic patterns, we identify where additional nutritional support may be beneficial and which areas are naturally optimised, helping refine dietary choices with greater precision.

02

Your metabolism and energy efficiency

Your genetic profile shows how your body converts food into usable energy. Variations in genes involved in mitochondrial function and nutrient breakdown influence how efficiently you produce and use energy throughout the day.

By analysing these pathways, we identify whether your body naturally tends toward faster or slower energy use, offering insight into performance capacity, recovery patterns, and endurance potential.

03

Your risk factors for certain health conditions

Your genetic data can indicate variations linked to conditions such as cardiovascular disease, diabetes, inflammation, or lipid imbalance. These markers help identify areas where your biology may need additional monitoring or support.

By analysing these risk factors early, our reports provide the foundation for preventive strategies and precise lifestyle adjustments that help maintain optimal biological balance over time.

04

How your body handles stress and inflammation

Your genes influence how your body reacts to stress, manages cortisol levels, and repairs after exposure to physical or emotional strain. Certain variations can make some people more sensitive to stress or slower to recover from inflammation.

By identifying these genetic markers, our analysis highlights how your body regulates immune and inflammatory responses, offering insights that support recovery, resilience, and balanced physiological function.

05

Your hormonal and sleep patterns

Your genes play a role in regulating hormones that control sleep cycles, energy levels, and overall rhythm. Variations in melatonin, cortisol, and circadian genes can influence how easily you fall asleep, how rested you feel, and how your body adapts to daily changes.

By examining these genetic pathways, our analysis provides insight into your natural biological rhythm and how to align your lifestyle for improved rest, focus, and recovery.

06

Your cellular repair and ageing mechanisms

Your genes influence how effectively your cells repair DNA damage, maintain telomere length, and sustain healthy function over time. Variations in these pathways affect how quickly your cells renew and how resilient they remain under stress.

By analysing these ageing mechanisms, our reports highlight opportunities to strengthen cellular maintenance, delay biological ageing, and support long term vitality at the molecular level.

07

Your detoxification and antioxidant capacity

Your genes determine how efficiently your body neutralises toxins, processes environmental compounds, and defends against oxidative stress. Variations in these pathways can influence how well your liver enzymes and antioxidant systems function.

Through this analysis, you can see how your detoxification capacity operates at the molecular level and where additional nutritional or lifestyle support may strengthen cellular protection and recovery.

08

Your mental health and cognitive tendencies

Your genetic makeup influences how neurotransmitters such as dopamine and serotonin are produced and regulated, affecting focus, emotional stability, and stress response. These variations help explain differences in mood balance, attention, and cognitive flexibility.

By analysing these pathways, our reports reveal how your brain processes information and adapts to stress, guiding strategies that support focus, resilience, and performance.

09

Your genetic resilience and longevity potential

Your genes influence how your body maintains stability, repairs damage, and adapts with age. Variations in these pathways affect how efficiently your cells recover from stress and sustain long term function.

By examining these genetic indicators, our analysis uncovers how your biology supports resilience and longevity, offering a deeper view of the mechanisms that drive healthy ageing.

Good news

The most advanced technologyIn human history.

With whole genome sequencing we are closer than ever to understanding what makes each of us unique. By analysing your complete genome, we reveal the biological patterns that define how your body functions and adapts over time.

Whole genome sequencing, as its name suggests, is a scientific method that lets us read and analyse your entire genetic code.

Every cell in your body contains DNA, which acts like an instruction manual for how your body works. This technology looks at all three billion letters of that manual, not just small parts of it.

By sequencing the whole genome, we can see every piece of genetic information that makes you who you are, helping us understand health, traits, and potential risks in a much deeper and more complete way.
